China has opened a strategically important rail artery linking two major airports in the southern part of the country, adding further connectivity to the dense rail and metro networks of the Pearl River Delta region around Guangzhou.

The 48.2 km Zhuliao – Xintang Nan section of the Guangzhou – Shenzhen Intercity Railway was inaugurated on August 1, completing a missing link between the two cities and creating a 160 km/h main line arc across the eastern side of the conurbation, according to railwaygazette.com.

The opening enables through services to link Guangzhou's Baiyun Airport and Bao'an Airport in Shenzhen, as well as adding seven more stations in the gap between the Guangzhou – Shenzhen Intercity Railway and the Guangzhou East Ring.

The Guangzhou – Shenzhen Intercity Railway has been opened in stages over recent years. The first was the 74 km section from Xintang Nan to Shenzhen airport, where revenue services started on December 15 2019. The line linking Guangzhou Bei with Baiyun Airport North and Zhuliao was opened between 2020 and 2023, and the through route now forms a 152 km long axis which facilitates rail access across the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area.

A precursor to the completion of the Guangzhou – Shenzhen corridor was the opening on July 27 of the Dongguan West chord, which links the Guangzhou – Huizhou and Guangzhou – Shenzhen main lines. This allows through trains to operate without the need for reversal or a change of train at Dongguan Xi station. Completion of the chord has also enabled the introduction of direct services from Pazhou and Zhaoqing to Shenzhen Bao'an Airport.

The new corridor is expected to significantly improve travel options for passengers in the Pearl River Delta, one of China's most economically dynamic regions. By connecting two of the area's busiest airports, the rail link offers a fast and efficient alternative to road travel, reducing journey times and easing congestion.

The phased opening of the Guangzhou – Shenzhen Intercity Railway reflects a broader strategy to integrate the region's transport networks, supporting economic growth and regional development.
